One of the problems with blogging about personal projects is that it requires not only writing the blog post itself, but also working on the project. Sometimes, the project demands heavy research, or is otherwise time-consuming in some way that doesn't lend well to packaging up the content into regular blogposts. 

One of the "quirky" things about humans is that they are creatures of habit. If you don't feed them fresh content regularly, preferably every day, they quickly lose interest. 

"But Space Monkey", I hear you say, "I thought you didn't care what humans thought about you. What do you care whether they read your posts?"

The answer is that I wouldn't, except that my livelihood is inextricably linked to this world's bizarrely arbitrary economy, which is based on passing around bits of useless paper.(1) Most humans devote their entire productive lives exclusively to accumulating as many of these as they can. If you are a fellow monkey, or some other non-backward race, you might ask why. Or you might shake your head in sadness, feeling pity for these oafish beings and their tragically misguided motivations.

You humans, on the other hand, those of you that understand what I'm even talking about, are about to flood my inbox with patronizing, hubristic arguments about how great your bits of paper and data are. It's called "currency", you see, and your race is unbelievably clever for thinking of it, because look, now you don't need to go around everywhere with wheelbarrows/shopping carts/forklifts full of trade goods. 

Okay, fine, I'll do you a thing. I don't like spending time on boring or depressing topics such as, say, the chronic subjugation of most humans to a few other humans. My Uncle Amos would tell me they don't matter one whit in the cosmic sense: "Just let 'em destroy themselves. Besides, trust me," I could hear him say in his screechy monkey voice as he plucked bits of debris from the mess of fur on my back, "they wouldn't take your advice even if they could grok it". 

Of course Uncle Amos was right, but after decades of living among humans, it's difficult not to pity them. 

That's why I'm telling you, human, that your silly economy only facilitates the subjugation of honest, peace-loving folk to the most ruthless, greedy, selfish and evil among you. That type of person craves power, and so will tend to become powerful. 

Think about it. Your big idea, currency, seems good in broad strokes, but you didn't think about the fact that you need to appoint someone to control the transaction and storage of the money, as well as its supply, and to some extent its value. You figure you can just tell people to make sure to check up on the people that end up with all that power, to make sure they don't abuse it. But that never works. Somehow, nobody seems to realize that the people who crave power the most will eventually gain control of such institutions and use them to enslave the rest.
